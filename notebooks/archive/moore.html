1. It appears to me
      1. that in Ethics, as in all other philosophical studies, the difficulties and disagreements,
        1. of which its history is full,
      2. are mainly due to a very simple cause: namely to the attempt
        1. to answer questions,
        2. without first discovering precisely what question
          1. it
        3. is
          1. which
          2. you desire
            1. to answer.
    1. I do not know how far this source of error would be done away,
      1. if philosophers would try
        1. to discover what question
          1. they were asking,
        2. before they set about
          1. to answer it;
      2. for the work of analysis and distinction is often very difficult:
        1. we may often fail
          1. to make the necessary discovery,
          2. even though we make a definite attempt
            1. to do so.
    1. But I am inclined
      1. to think
        1. that in many cases a resolute attempt would be sufficient
          1. to ensure success;
          2. so that,
            1. if only this attempt were made,
          3. many of the most glaring difficulties and disagreements in philosophy would disap pear.
    1. At all events, philosophers seem, in general,
      1. not to make the attempt,
    2. and, whether in consequence of this omission or not, they are constantly endeavoring
      1. to prove
        1. that 'Yes' or 'No' will answer questions,
          1. to which neither answer is correct,
          2. owing to the fact
            1. that what
              1. they have before their minds
            2. is not one question, but several, to some of which
              1. the true answer is 'No', to others 'Yes'.
